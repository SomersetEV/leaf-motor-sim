## 1. The logger still drops most frames

Every stream holds about 9–12 % of its frames, in bursts of about 0.23 s every 1.6–2 s, with 21,714 `0x4A1` frames missing. This is the same pattern as the 7 Nov logs (V6), so the logger on the tractor is not running the 10 Nov fix, or the fix does not hold in run mode. So `extract_params.py` cannot run on this log: its 5 Hz filter needs 0.3 s of clean data either side of each sample. The fit below works burst by burst instead.

## 2. Inertia and friction (neutral, PTO out)

The fit is `T = J dω/dt + Tc + b ω` over every sample above 300 rpm, with the slope from a 70 ms window inside each burst. It uses reported torque (`0x1DA`; see section 4 for why).

| Data | J kg·m² | Tc Nm | b Nm·s/rad | rms Nm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1/R2, reported torque | 0.057 | 4.3 | 0.049 | 1.1 |
| R1/R2, requested torque | 0.061 | 4.7 | 0.046 | 1.4 |
| R4, reported torque | 0.054 | 3.5 | 0.048 | 1.4 |
| **All, reported torque** | **0.055** | **4.8** | **0.045** | 1.2 |
| Simulator default (placeholder) | 0.30 | 5 | 0.02 | |

R1/R2 and R4 agree within 5 % on J, even though R4 runs at five times the torque. A coast check fits too: at 256 s the measured slowdown is 1290 rpm/s, and the fit predicts 1420 rpm/s.

What this means:
- Friction is about **13 Nm at 1800 rpm** (4.8 Nm plus 4.7 Nm per 1000 rpm), and `c` comes out 0.
- J is **5.5 times smaller** than the simulator's placeholder. It is about what a Leaf rotor plus the gearbox input shaft should be.

### Why 2000 rpm was hard to hold

None of this is a fault. 2.8 % pedal is about 14 Nm, which equals the friction at about 1900 rpm. Each 0.1 % of pedal (0.5 Nm) moves the balance point by about **110 rpm**. Any torque above friction speeds the shaft up at about **175 rpm/s per Nm**. Holding 2000 rpm by foot means holding the pedal to ±0.05 %.

The field sheet's "3 to 5 s to reach 2000 rpm" assumed a much heavier shaft, so it could never work, and R1 does not need exactly 2000 anyway. The holds at 1200, 1600, 1750 and 1880 rpm are what pinned down the friction line (`friction.png`). The coasts are short: from 1800 rpm the shaft stops in under a second, so each coast caught at most one burst.

## 3. R4: at Kp 11 the governor hunts and overshoots

See `r4_bursts.png`.

- **First hit (401.6 s, pedal 18 %):** speed reached **3460 rpm with `revlim` 2200**, 1260 rpm over. At 6600 rpm/s, the shift-4 speed filter (155 ms lag) ran up to 1000 rpm behind, so the governor did not start to cut torque until the shaft was already at 3130 rpm.
- **Holding the pedal at 9.5–12 %:** speed swings between about **1600 and 2470 rpm**. It is a sustained oscillation, not a settling ring.
- **`loopcalc.py` at the measured J and b** predicts this. At Kp 11 the loop is unstable at every filter shift: phase margin −42° at shift 4, −55° at shift 2. The largest stable P-only gain in neutral is about:

  | `govFlt` | Largest stable `govKp` (neutral) | Ring at that gain |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 4 | about 2 | 0.63 s, keeps 0.56 per cycle |
  | 2 | about 3 | 0.32 s, keeps 0.75 |
  | 1 | about 4 | 0.24 s, keeps 0.89 |

  `govKd` 1 did not help in any of these.
- **Context:** neutral with the PTO out is the lightest the shaft ever gets. With the PTO and an implement engaged, J is much larger, so this does not say Kp 11 is wrong for baling. It does say that the governor will hunt by ±300–400 rpm whenever the tractor sits at `revlim` with nothing engaged.
- **The bench `noload` preset was J 0.30 until 14 Nov, which does not represent this condition.** The V5 no-load ring numbers from M4 and M5a were all taken at J 0.30.

### Safety: do not repeat R4 as the sheet wrote it

The field sheet says to snap the throttle *fully open*. Ben used 9–18 %, which was the right call. At 18 % the shaft still overshot by 1260 rpm. Full pedal requests up to 512 Nm: the shaft would reach the limit in tens of milliseconds and keep accelerating for the whole filter lag, which could mean several thousand rpm over in neutral. The field sheet was rewritten on 14 Nov (section 7).

## 4. The inverter delivered about half the torque in fast transients

At low, steady torque the reported torque matches the request (ratio 0.95–1.02 below 25 Nm), as it did in the 7 Nov logs. In R4 it does not:
- Requests of 48–61 Nm gave **20–37 Nm**, and 92 Nm gave about 55 Nm.
- Small regen requests (−5 to −12 Nm) while slowing gave about **0 Nm**.
- The shortfall always opposes the change in speed.

The accelerations follow the reported torque: J from R4 matches J from R1/R2 only on reported torque, while the request gives 0.11–0.2 kg·m². So the shortfall is real.

The shortfall correlates with acceleration (about −0.05 Nm per rad/s², r² 0.5), but not cleanly. It may be damping or a rate limit inside the inverter. `0x1DC` (battery limits) is constant and the same as on 7 Nov, so that is not the cause. The bursts are too short to settle it, so it needs a continuous log.

It matters for the bench: the simulator delivers the full request after the 30 ms lag, so its R4 would be harsher than the tractor's. It also adds damping that helps the real loop. **Proposed new plan item V9.**

## 5. Smaller things

- **Reverse spin after release:** with `regenmax` −20 and the pedal released, the shaft was driven backwards twice, to −185 rpm (414.9 s) and −88 rpm (428.8 s). This is because the light shaft passes through zero under regen. In reverse the Zombie's request flips sign, but the inverter delivered about 0 Nm. It is harmless in neutral and irrelevant in gear, but it is worth knowing.
- **Junk speed at power-up:** the first `0x1DA` frames read 16383 rpm (t = 152 s). Any tool fitting from the start of a session should skip them; `analyse_r1r2r4.py` starts at 206 s.
